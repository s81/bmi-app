Methodology

This tool is for informational purposes only and does not replace clinical assessment.

{{ section_label("Validated Indices") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("Standard BMI", "WHO, 1995") | safe }}
$$\text{BMI} = \frac{\text{weight (kg)}}{\text{height (m)}^2}$$
{{ formula_card_close("The foundational index proposed by WHO for population-level obesity screening. Defined as body weight divided by the square of height. [1]") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("New BMI — Peterson Formula", "Peterson et al., 2016") | safe }}
$$\text{New BMI} = \frac{1.3 \times \text{weight (kg)}}{\text{height (m)}^{2.5}}$$
{{ formula_card_close("Corrects the standard formula's systematic bias against tall individuals by raising the height exponent from 2 to 2.5. Uses the same WHO classification thresholds as Standard BMI. [2]") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("Ponderal Index", "Rohrer, 1921") | safe }}
$$\text{PI} = \frac{\text{weight (kg)}}{\text{height (m)}^3}$$
{{ formula_card_close("An alternative slenderness measure that is less sensitive to height variation than BMI. Normal physiological range: 11–14 kg/m³. [3]") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("Body Surface Area — Mosteller Formula", "Mosteller, 1987") | safe }}
$$\text{BSA} = \sqrt{\frac{\text{height (cm)} \times \text{weight (kg)}}{3600}}$$
{{ formula_card_close("Used clinically for chemotherapy dosing, cardiac output normalisation, and renal function assessment. Reported in m². [4]") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("Waist-to-Height Ratio", "Ashwell & Gibson, 2016") | safe }}
$$\text{WHtR} = \frac{\text{waist (cm)}}{\text{height (cm)}}$$
{{ formula_card_close("A dimensionless index that adjusts waist circumference for stature. Stronger predictor of cardiometabolic risk than BMI alone because it captures central adiposity directly. The evidence-based target is WHtR < 0.5 ('keep your waist to less than half your height'). [6]") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("Ideal Body Weight — Hamwi Formula", "Hamwi, 1964") | safe }}
$$\text{IBW} = \begin{cases} 48.0 + 2.7 \times (h_{\text{in}} - 60) & \text{male} \\ 45.5 + 2.2 \times (h_{\text{in}} - 60) & \text{female} \end{cases}$$
{{ formula_card_close("The original IBW formula, developed for insulin dosing in diabetes management. Height in inches above 5 feet (60 in). Result in kg. [10]") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("Ideal Body Weight — Devine Formula", "Devine, 1974") | safe }}
$$\text{IBW} = \begin{cases} 50.0 + 2.3 \times (h_{\text{in}} - 60) & \text{male} \\ 45.5 + 2.3 \times (h_{\text{in}} - 60) & \text{female} \end{cases}$$
{{ formula_card_close("The most widely used IBW formula in clinical practice, originally developed for gentamicin dosing. Remains the standard reference in pharmacokinetics. [11]") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("Ideal Body Weight — Robinson Formula", "Robinson et al., 1983") | safe }}
$$\text{IBW} = \begin{cases} 52.0 + 1.9 \times (h_{\text{in}} - 60) & \text{male} \\ 49.0 + 1.7 \times (h_{\text{in}} - 60) & \text{female} \end{cases}$$
{{ formula_card_close("Developed as a refinement to the Devine formula with a smaller per-inch increment. Used for drug dosage calculations and mechanical ventilation tidal volume settings. [12]") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("Ideal Body Weight — Miller Formula", "Miller et al., 1983") | safe }}
$$\text{IBW} = \begin{cases} 56.2 + 1.41 \times (h_{\text{in}} - 60) & \text{male} \\ 53.1 + 1.36 \times (h_{\text{in}} - 60) & \text{female} \end{cases}$$
{{ formula_card_close("Higher base weight with a smaller per-inch increment than other formulas, resulting in higher IBW estimates for shorter and lower for taller individuals. [13]") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("Body Fat % — U.S. Navy Method", "Hodgdon & Beckett, 1984") | safe }}
$$\%\text{BF}_{\text{male}} = \frac{495}{1.0324 - 0.19077\log_{10}(W - N) + 0.15456\log_{10}(H)} - 450$$
$$\%\text{BF}_{\text{female}} = \frac{495}{1.29579 - 0.35004\log_{10}(W + P - N) + 0.22100\log_{10}(H)} - 450$$
{{ formula_card_close("W = waist (cm) · N = neck (cm) · P = hip (cm, female only) · H = height (cm). Validated on U.S. military personnel; widely used as a no-equipment field estimate. Accuracy ±3–4% compared to hydrostatic weighing. [7]") | safe }} {{ formula_card_open("Body Fat % — Deurenberg BMI Method", "Deurenberg et al., 1991") | safe }}
$$\%\text{BF} = 1.20 \times \text{BMI} + 0.23 \times \text{age} - 10.8 \times S - 5.4$$
{{ formula_card_close("S = 1 for male, 0 for female. Requires no additional measurements beyond BMI and age. Less accurate than circumference-based methods (SEE ≈ 4.1%), but useful when anthropometric measurements are unavailable. [8]") | safe }}
